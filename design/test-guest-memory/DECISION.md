## Decision

`tools/run.py`, `tools/check-boot-stack.py`, `tools/measure-pinned.py` and `bench/run-bench.sh` boot a 2.5 GiB guest (`MEMORY_MIB`, 2560). The loader identity-maps the first 4 GiB, which is this guest's RAM and the 32-bit MMIO window just below 4 GiB, with 2M leaves; the kernel's image and its stack guard have 4K entries. Its page-table pool is 512 KiB, the kernel window is 8 MiB, dom0's window is 16 MiB with a 1 MiB stack, and its bump allocator is those two windows, 24 MiB. The loader hands the kernel its memory map as ranges of base, bytes and type, at most 256 of them, and the kernel tracks the pages of physical addresses 0 to 4 GiB: `NUM_PAGES` is 1,048,576, tiled by 2,048 2M regions and four 1G regions. A tracked page that no range calls available, in a hole, a device window or a firmware reservation, is never handed out, and memory a range names at or past 4 GiB is left unused. On the PC RAM starts at 0, the guest's is 0 to 2.5 GiB, and the second 1G region, 1 to 2 GiB, reaches the root as one untyped of order 18 at `0x40000000`. On QEMU virt devices sit below 1 GiB and RAM starts there, the guest's is 1 to 3.5 GiB, and the whole block is the third region, 2 to 3 GiB, at `0x80000000`. The boot check (`boot_serial_accepted` in `build-tool/src/command/test.rs`) takes the target and requires the kernel's line for that target's block; the gate's boots are the PC's. The loader's stack and the kernel's boot stack are 1 MiB each. The kernel keeps four CPU slots, each with 64 KiB interrupt and syscall stacks, each over a 4 KiB guard page. The benchmark starts four CPUs; the gate starts two.

## Context

The guest had been 32 GiB, then 6 GiB. Both were chosen from outside the kernel: 32 GiB was more than `NUM_PAGES` can track, and 6 GiB was what still fitted an Orange Pi after leaving the board's OS about a gigabyte. The loader meanwhile required one conventional region larger than a 2 GiB allocator, whether or not the guest used that allocator.

## Reasoning

The sizes are the bytes each pool has to hold, with slack where a check demands it.

- The memory map crosses to the kernel as typed ranges, which is the shape firmware gives it in, on x86_64 and on aarch64. OVMF lists 30 regions for the test guest once the loader has labelled its own; with their edges moved to pages and neighbours of one type merged they are 21 ranges of 24 bytes, in a list that holds 256. The list's length does not depend on how much memory there is, so the loader does not know how many pages the kernel tracks: the kernel checks the ranges at the trust boundary, takes the free pages below its own limit, and leaves the rest unused.
- The stacks are sized from what is written on them, measured by zeroing each, booting to dom0's exit and dumping it with `pmemsave`. The kernel's boot writes 48 KiB of its stack in a debug build and 16 KiB in release; the stack is 1 MiB, and `tools/check-boot-stack.py` requires 512 KiB of it unused. The overflow build's stack is 8 KiB, under that depth in either profile and over what `main` uses before the IDT exists. The loader's `load` writes 108 KiB of its stack in a debug build and 48 KiB in release, with the handoff and its own copies of the memory map on it; that stack is 1 MiB.
- Sixteen CPU slots with eight 1 MiB stacks each were 128 MiB of kernel image. Four slots with 64 KiB stacks are 2 MiB. Boot's copy of the handoff is on the boot stack, not on these.
- The test guest's RAM ends at 2.5 GiB on the PC and at 3.5 GiB on virt. On the PC the local APIC, the I/O APIC and the IOMMU sit just below 4 GiB, and the loader only allocates below 4 GiB, so the identity map ends at 4 GiB. With 2M leaves that map is six tables: a PML4, a PDPT and four page directories. With 4K entries it is a page table for every 2 MiB besides, 2,054 tables and about 8 MiB. `tools/run.py`'s CPU model, Haswell-IBRS, has no 1G pages.
- A 2M stretch has a page table only where something inside it needs a protection of its own. The kernel write-protects one page of its image at a time (`write_protect.rs` under `kernel/src/arch/<target>/`), and its 1 MiB stack guard is less than a stretch, so the loader gives the stretches from the start of the kernel's image to the end of the guard 4K entries. The rest of the kernel window, the stack included, and all other memory stay on 2M leaves.
- The page-table pool is 128 pages, 512 KiB. The loader logs how many hold tables as it enters the kernel, the line `Page-table pool:` in `serial.log` after `tools/run.py`: 16 in the debug build and 15 in release (`tools/check-boot-stack.py release`, `target/boot-stack-check-release/serial.log`). Those are 6 for the identity map, 4 page tables for the kernel's image and guard in debug and 3 in release, and 6 for dom0: a PDPT, a page directory and two page tables for its image and stack, and a page directory and a page table for the payload. The most the loader can write is 24: 6, then 5 for a kernel image at its 6 MiB bound and the guard, 7 MiB that touch five stretches at most wherever they start, and 13 for a 16 MiB dom0 window, which is a PDPT, two page directories and ten page tables. That fits the lower 64 pages, so the upper half, which the boot-stack check requires to stay unwritten, stays clear.
- The kernel window holds the image, then the 1 MiB guard and the 1 MiB stack. With the CPU slots and the boot scratch the image is 3.62 MiB in a debug build and 3.32 MiB in release, the end of its last `LOAD` segment in `readelf -lW` of `target/x86_64-unknown-aloader/{debug,release}/kernel`. The window allows it 6 MiB, which leaves 2.38 MiB to grow into, so the window is 8 MiB; an image past the bound stops the loader where it allocates. dom0's release image is under 1 MiB, so its window is 16 MiB with a 1 MiB stack. The bump allocator is those two windows and nothing over, 24 MiB, since they are all the loader takes from it, and the guest's largest conventional region is larger than that without the 2 GiB floor. What the windows do not cover stays conventional memory and reaches the kernel as free.

- The capability kernel keeps about 48 bytes per tracked page (a 32-byte page entry, an 8-byte stale epoch, an 8-byte zeroing cursor), and boot zeroes that stretch. Tracking 4 GiB is 12,323 pages with the root's boot objects, 48.1 MiB, and one byte per tracked page of boot scratch in the kernel's image, 1 MiB.
- A 1G block is one of the sizes the block table types, and a boot has to be able to produce one for anything to exercise it. The first 1G region of RAM cannot be it: on the PC it holds page 0, the legacy hole below 1 MiB, the firmware's low reservations around 8 MiB, the loader's windows and the kernel's image, and on virt the firmware's low reservations and the same windows. The last cannot either, since the firmware allocates from the top of RAM down. So the guest has a whole 1G region of RAM between them, with 512 MiB above it for the firmware.
- The kernel tracks from address 0 because a page's index is its address over 4 KiB everywhere in `kernel/verified`: a capability's base, a table entry's address and a block's region are all computed from it. The tracked range therefore has to reach the end of RAM on the machine whose RAM starts highest. Virt's 2.5 GiB end at 3.5 GiB, so the range is 4 GiB on both targets, one constant with one proof. On virt the first quarter of it, the device addresses below 1 GiB, holds no RAM, which costs 12 MiB of the stretch.
- Measured on a boot of this guest (`tools/run.py`, debug build, the loader's `region.base()` lines in `serial.log`): the loader's kernel window is `0x1780000` to `0x1f7ffff` and dom0's window `0x1f80000` to `0x2f7ffff`, both below 48 MiB. One conventional range runs from `0x2f80000` to `0x9bb5bfff` and holds all of 1 to 2 GiB. The firmware's lowest allocation above it is at `0x9bb5c000`, 443 MiB past 2 GiB; the loader's page-table pool (`0x9d5ef000`), its image and the firmware's ACPI and NVS ranges follow, up to `0x9fffffff`, with conventional memory between them. The kernel puts the capability kernel at `0x2f80000`, the first pages past dom0's window, where it ends at `0x5fa2fff`. What is left folds to 7 free runs and 48 aligned blocks, 2,431 MiB, all of which the root takes: one each of order 18, 17, 16, 15 and 13 from `0x6000000` to `0x7fffffff`, one each of order 16, 15, 13, 12 and 11 from `0x80000000` to `0x9b7fffff`, and thirty-eight of order 12 and below from the gaps under 96 MiB and between the firmware's allocations above `0x9b800000`. The gate's boots, with and without the IOMMU, fail without the kernel's line `untyped base 0x40000000 order 18`.
- Measured on virt with the firmware alone (`tools/orangepi-kvm.py --memory-mib 2560 --esp` with a directory whose `startup.nsh` runs the UEFI shell's `memmap`, EDK2 under KVM on an Orange Pi 5): RAM is `0x40000000` to `0xdfffffff`. The firmware holds `0x44000000` to `0x4401ffff` and most of `0x477d9000` to `0x47ffffff`, with 64 MiB of conventional memory below the first and 56 MiB between them. One conventional range runs from `0x48000000` to `0xde8d7fff` and holds all of 2 to 3 GiB. From `0xde8d8000`, 489 MiB past 3 GiB, to the end of RAM the firmware holds all but scraps. The loader takes its 24 MiB of windows from the first conventional range that holds them and the kernel its 48 MiB stretch from the first free run that does, both in the first GiB of RAM, so the kernel's line for the block is to be `untyped base 0x80000000 order 18`.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Keep 6 GiB so the guest matches the Orange Pi.** The board is not the test. A guest that leaves the board's OS a gigabyte was a host constraint, and it hid the loader pools.
- **Stay at 32 GiB.** All but the first 4 GiB would be RAM the kernel does not track.
- **Track 2 GiB.** It covers the PC's block at half the metadata. Virt's RAM starts at 1 GiB, so 2 GiB of tracked addresses hold 1 GiB of its RAM, with the kernel's image, dom0's window and the capability kernel inside it: no boot there has a whole 1G block, and the root gets under 1 GiB in all.
- **Track from a physical base the target names.** It spares virt the 12 MiB for the addresses below its RAM. A page's index would stop being its address over 4 KiB, which the page arithmetic of `kernel/verified` assumes in every module that names a block, and each target would verify a different constant.
- **Keep 2 GiB and drop the 1G check on virt.** One target would then never show that boot can hand out a 1G block, which is what the check is for.
- **A 512 MiB guest with 1 GiB tracked.** It boots, but its one 1G region is never free, so no boot produces a 1G block.
- **A 2 GiB guest.** The firmware allocates from the top of RAM, which would then be inside the second 1G region.
- **Hand the kernel one entry per page.** At 16 bytes an entry that is 4 MiB for each GiB the list covers, copied by value onto the loader's stack and the kernel's, and a slot count the loader and the kernel must share at compile time.

## What this does not settle

RAM at or above 4 GiB is not tracked; a machine with RAM there needs a larger `NUM_PAGES`, and nothing in the loader changes with it. Nothing reserves either machine's free region: the loader allocates where the firmware's map leaves room, and a boot's check is what notices if that moves. The 1G block is an untyped; retype makes no 1G frame of it until the page table module maps one (design/capability-names-a-block/). A guest with RAM above 4 GiB needs an identity map past the 32-bit MMIO window; this machine has no such RAM. The kernel's image is on 4K entries for the write-protect check alone; a check that split the leaf it tests would let the image share 2M leaves with the rest.
