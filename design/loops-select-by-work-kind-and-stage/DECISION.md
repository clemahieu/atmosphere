## Decision

One driver script, `tools/loop.py`, inspects Beads in Python at zero token cost and dispatches sessions across harnesses (`claude`, `agy` and `grok`) and model difficulties from `loop.toml` at the project root.

One invocation is a pool. The pool process runs no session; it keeps `[pool] jobs` worker loops running, each a `tools/loop.py --worker <pool pid>` process, and re-reads the number every few seconds, so concurrency is changed by editing `loop.toml` rather than by opening or closing terminals. Each worker is a whole loop as before: its own Beads actor `loop-<pid>`, its own pid file under `logs/loop/` naming its pool, and the same claim, recovery and wait rules toward its siblings as toward any other loop. The pool passes stop, stop-now and reload on to its workers, stops them all when one finds nothing left but questions, and does not replace a worker that failed until the number changes.

Each worker routes its work by `[offerings.<name>]` and `[routes.<key>]`. An offering is one way to run a session: its `harness`, `model`, `effort` and `autocompact`, and `meters`, the usage meters it draws on (Fable draws on `claude-week` and its own `claude-fable`; a Gemini model on `gemini-week` and `gemini-5h`). Each of the twelve work and stage keys — leaf kinds (`tool`, `sys`, `proof`, `spec`, `arch`, `review`), epic rounds (`epic-close`, `epic-split`), post-prover landing (`needs-land`), and specialist or escalation passes (`prover`, `skeptic`, `retry`), with `[routes.default]` as fallback — has a route holding only `candidates`, the offerings able to do that work, cheapest capable first, or `disabled = true`. At claim, and for each hand-off, the work goes to the first candidate free to take it: none of its meters is cooled down, and each of its paced meters admits one more session than the loops have in flight on it. When no candidate is free the work is not claimed and waits, for the earliest reset when only cooldowns keep it and for a minute at most when a pace does, and `--check` names each cooldown and pace it waits on. A route naming an offering that is not defined is a configuration error, so the loop keeps the last good file. One `tools/loop.py` invocation determines both what is ready next and which offering runs it.

Tasks are classified by what the work is and what stage it has reached, never by a model's name:

- **Work kinds** (`kind:<name>` on new leaves, with `--no-inherit-labels`, or inferred deterministically for unlabeled leaves): `tool` (tier-3 scripts, hooks, UI and docs), `sys` (unverified Rust, platform and boot glue), `proof` (proving an existing tier-1 specification, lemma, witness or `external_body` removal), `spec` (a new tier-1 specification or operation), `arch` (`design/` records and spikes), and `review` (epic skeptic-review leaves, `design/review-is-a-leaf-of-its-epic/`).
- **Stage hand-offs** (`stage:<name>`): `stage:needs-prover` (a round wrote `Prover input:` and the `prover` route is disabled or every candidate is cooled down), `stage:needs-skeptic` (a round matched a review's evidence and wrote `Skeptic input`, and the `skeptic` route is disabled or cooled down), `stage:escalated` (a leaf's first attempt reached its ten-run bound, and the `retry` route is disabled or cooled down), and `stage:needs-land` (a specialist prover finished in the leaf's worktree, leaving triage, the gate and closing).

Before spawning a session, a routed loop inspects Beads in Python at zero token cost: it recovers abandoned `in_progress` issues from dead loops, claims a queued `stage:needs-prover` or `stage:needs-skeptic` issue and invokes that specialist agent directly without a wrapper round, claims a matching `stage:escalated`, `stage:needs-land` or `kind:*` leaf and pins the round prompt to that issue (`Issue <id> is yours`), or starts an epic-only round when `tools/reviews.py` lists finished epics. When nothing in Beads matches, no LLM session is started: the loop waits while another loop or actor is active, and exits cleanly otherwise.

Rate and usage limits are tracked per meter in one state file, `logs/loop/state.json`, that the pool and every worker share, written under a lock and replaced atomically. A limit cools the meter the session's stream names, or every meter the offering declares when the stream names none; when the meter that refused is not among the offering's declared ones, the whole harness is cooled as well, since the declaration missed it. The unrouted loop's top-level tables declare no meters, so their limit cools the whole harness. The cooldown one worker writes makes every worker pass over each offering that draws on that meter until its reset, without spending a session on finding the limit again: a leaf falls through to the next candidate free to take it, any pre-claimed issue is released keeping its stage label, a specialist hand-off with no free candidate parks on `stage:needs-prover`, `stage:needs-skeptic`, or `stage:escalated`, and ready work whose candidates are free continues immediately. The same file keeps each provider's meters: the pool samples Claude's every five minutes with `tools/claude-usage.py` (the week, the five-hour window when the plan has one, and each scoped model limit) and Gemini's from `agy -p /usage`, workers add the readings Claude sessions stream, and each meter keeps its readings history and a rate in percent per hour fitted over the recent readings, split at resets, so pacing sessions to a meter's reset has its rate to work from and the history outlives a restart of the pool.

Every session a loop runs, on any harness, appends one row to `logs/loop/sessions.sqlite` as it ends: its issue and the work kind, classified at claim or classified the same way when the session names an issue this loop has not classified, the route and offering it was chosen by, the harness, model and effort, its start, minutes, turns, plan-weighted tokens, exit, whether it hit a usage limit, and a round's report words. The loop is the one place that sees every session with the offering it ran on, so the comparison of offerings per work kind reads this record rather than each harness's own transcripts. `tools/offering-report.py` gives it with the tracker. For each work kind and the offering that took a leaf, the leaves taken, landed, landed with no attempt ending at its bound, escalated to a retry and served an epic whose review then closed refuted, and the weighted tokens and plan points a landed leaf cost, every session on it counted. A session whose issue is an epic now stays with the leaf kind and offering the row recorded when the issue was taken. Review work is its own rows, one per offering that took a review: taken, closed on a skeptic pass, closed refuted, and the cost of every session on the review. Epic rounds and the unrouted loop's rounds, which name no leaf, follow by offering. Every session is on exactly one of those lines, and the lines sum to the record. The human orders a route's candidates from that table. A grok offering's cost there is the weighted-token column. The plan is a flat subscription, so a grok session has no weekly points and is not listed as unrated. The API-equivalent dollars Grok stamps on a session (`costUsdTicks`, 10^10 a dollar) are list price, which is not the unit, for the same reason a Claude session is priced in weekly points rather than list dollars. `tools/token-ledger.py` counts a Grok session from the token buckets that stamp, and `tools/leaf-cost.py` includes those sessions in a leaf's round count without adding weekly points.

Concurrency follows the meters rather than the number of workers. `[meters.<name>]` gives a meter a `target`, the percent its projected use at the reset stays under (95 leaves room for interactive use), and a `max`, the most sessions it admits at once. A meter at p percent, its reset h hours away, admits floor((target − p) / (h × r)) sessions, clamped to [0, max], where r is the meter's fitted rate over the mean number of the loops' sessions in flight on it at the readings the rate was fitted over, a mean under one counting as one. Each reading records that number, which every worker's pid file gives by naming the meters its session draws on. A meter at or past its target admits none; one with no readings, no rate yet, or a window that has reset since its reading admits its max; a meter no table names is not paced. An offering is free only when every meter it draws on has room, so Fable waits on both the week and its own meter, and the five-hour window binds alongside the week. The pool recomputes every paced meter's admission each tick and logs each change with p, h and r; each worker recomputes it before it chooses, so admission never lags a stale file. The reset reckoned is the scheduled one: a reset the provider grants early is not budgeted, and the lowered reading it leaves is room the pool then spends.

For `harness = "agy"` and `harness = "grok"`, `tools/loop.py` strips the YAML frontmatter from `.claude/agents/<kind>.md` and passes the procedure body with the prompt to `agy -p` or `grok -p`, keeping `.claude/agents/` the single source of truth across harnesses. A Grok session streams in Claude's wire format, so one reader follows both, and gets the project root's `CLAUDE.md` as extra rules, since Grok does not look above the repository. Grok prints its plan meter only in its interactive session, so `tools/grok-usage.py` starts one in a private tmux server, types `/usage` and reads the weekly percent and reset off the window; the pool asks it every fifteen minutes and keeps the reading as `grok-week`, paced like any other meter. A session that ends on a limit names no meter or reset, so it cools the offering's meters for an hour.

## Context

Running every round, retry, proof search and skeptic pass on one Anthropic subscription saturated the weekly meter before the week ended, leaving ready leaves unworked while a Google AI Ultra subscription (`agy` with Gemini 4 Carbon) sat idle. Meanwhile, inside the Anthropic allowance itself, Fable 5.2 cost 4.75 times as much per weighted token as Opus 5.5, so spending Fable on mechanical rounds, gating, or routine tool leaves starved the hard proof searches and escalations where Fable pays for itself.

An earlier experiment routed tier-3 issues to a separate model by adding a dedicated driver script, a duplicate procedure file, and a model-named tracker label (`grok`). It was removed a day later when that model proved unreliable on unsupervised rounds: the duplicate script and procedure had already drifted from `tools/loop.py` and `.claude/agents/round.md`, and the model-named label coupled tracker state to one vendor's product name.

## Reasoning

Measured on 2026-11-13 from `logs/token-ledger.sqlite` (`tools/token-ledger.py` and `tools/leaf-cost.py`) and one `bd list --all --json`:

- Across 774.2M weighted tokens recorded in the ledger, `round` accounted for 596.9M (77.1%), `skeptic` for 116.5M (15.0%), `prover` for 17.8M (2.3%), and interactive sessions for the remainder.
- Meter fittings in `tools/leaf-cost.py` put Opus 5.5 at 0.12 weekly points per million weighted tokens and Fable 5.2 at 0.57. A single Fable round of 2.5M weighted tokens costs 1.4 weekly points; reserving Fable for `stage:needs-prover`, `stage:escalated`, and open-oracle `spec` work preserves that budget for proof search while moving mechanical rounds off it.
- Across 631 closed leaves with ledger rows, 62% were tier-3 tooling, unverified platform glue, or closed-oracle tier-1 proofs that never invoked `prover`. Even within a tier-1 leaf that does invoke `prover`, reading, editing, running `tools/land.py` across the gate, and closing are distinct stages from the 25-run Verus search itself, which `stage:needs-prover` and `stage:needs-land` separate cleanly across loops.
- Checking Beads in Python before spawning a headless CLI session costs zero tokens when no route has a stage or leaf ready that it may take, instead of paying ~90K cached input tokens per idle `Loop: wait` check.

A route lists offerings rather than naming one because the meters, not the providers, are what run out, and they run out separately: Fable's scoped limit can refuse while the Claude week has room for Opus, and a Carbon cooldown leaves Sonnet and Opus free for the low-difficulty kinds Carbon would have taken. Ordering the candidates cheapest capable first spends the cheap meters before the dear ones, and leaving an offering off a route is how a route says which work a provider must never take, such as a specification or proof search no Gemini model is trusted with.

Concurrency is the lever that keeps the subscriptions under their limits. `[pool] jobs` is the ceiling, the number of workers; the meters' admission decides how many of them run a session on each meter at a time, so a meter ahead of its pace holds its offerings back while one behind lets them run, and the work an offering cannot take moves to the next candidate rather than waiting. Projecting to the reset rather than holding a fixed share an hour lets a meter near its reset spend what it would otherwise forfeit. Charging the rate to the loops' sessions, and counting fewer than one as one, keeps interactive use from reading as room: the projection errs toward fewer sessions. Workers stay separate processes, not threads of the pool, because the Beads actor is per process (the hook reads the pid), a session's process group is ended by its own loop's signal, and a worker that crashes takes no sibling with it.

Keeping one `tools/loop.py` binary preserves `loop_alive(pid)` (`/proc/<pid>/cmdline` ending in `tools/loop.py`), which `--stop`, `--reload`, peer-loop detection, and `.claude/agents/round.md` orient recovery all rely on.

## Alternatives rejected

- **One script per loop flavor (`tools/loop-carbon.py`, `tools/loop-fable.py`).** Duplicates signal handling, PID tracking, stream parsing, backoff and tests across files, and breaks `loop_alive(pid)` unless every recovery check learns every script name.
- **Model-named labels on issues (`carbon`, `fable`, `opus`).** Couples the issue tracker to today's model names and subscription tiers; every model release or quota shift requires relabeling open issues instead of editing `loop.toml`.
- **A second copy of `round.md` for `agy`.** Tried for the earlier Grok loop and drifted within a day. Passing the body of `.claude/agents/<kind>.md` directly to `agy -p` keeps one procedure.
- **One loop per slice of the work, each in its own terminal (a named profile listing the kinds, stages and epic rounds that loop takes and the hand-offs it leaves to others).** The pool runs every slice under one invocation, and a route's candidates already say which offerings may take each kind of work, so a slice is a second place to say what a model may take, and the concurrency it brings is the hand-started kind below.
- **Several loops started by hand, one per terminal.** Concurrency then changes only by starting or stopping terminals, so nothing in the script can adjust it to the meters, and stopping or reloading them all is a per-terminal chore.
- **Threads inside one loop process.** Every thread would share one pid and so one Beads actor, which breaks orient's rule that an issue in progress under your own actor was left by this loop.
- **One harness and model per route, with a harness-wide cooldown.** A tool leaf routed to Carbon then waits through a Carbon cooldown though Sonnet or Opus could do it, and one scoped limit, such as Fable's, stops every Claude route though the week still has room.
- **Letting the LLM check whether matching work exists.** Each idle wake-up loads rules and procedures into context only to emit `Loop: wait`. Querying `bd` in Python before starting a session makes idle specialist loops free.
- **Pricing a Grok session in the API-equivalent dollars Grok stamps on it.** Those dollars are list price. The Claude columns are weekly points because the meter does not charge by list price, and a dollar figure beside them would be a second unit. Weighted tokens are the quantity the session record already keeps for every harness, with one weighting, so a grok offering is compared on that column and has no weekly points.
- **Reading Claude's meters from `~/.claude.json`.** That file holds the interactive app's last fetch of the subscription's usage endpoint, and a pool of headless sessions does not fetch it, so the history stays one reading and the fitted rate stays absent. `tools/claude-usage.py` asks the endpoint, with the access token those sessions keep, on the same schedule as Gemini's.

## What this does not settle

- Whether Gemini 4 Carbon writes open-oracle tier-1 specifications (`kind:spec`) that survive the Opus skeptic at the same rate as Opus 5.5; `loop.toml` can keep `spec` on `fable` or `opus` until replayed leaves measure that rate.
