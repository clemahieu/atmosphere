# A thread is retyped into its process

## Decision

A thread is made in its process. The retype that types a thread's block names, besides the untyped and the destination slot, a slot holding a process capability with the control right. In one bounded step the block is typed, the thread's control capability is derived under the untyped into the destination slot, the thread's process binding, node 0 of its node page, is derived under the process capability, the process block gains one reference, and the thread's record names the process. Every refusal is readable before the block is typed: the refusals every retype has, a process slot that holds no live process capability with control, one at the depth limit, one whose block cannot take another reference, and, once dead targets are refused everywhere, a dead process. A refusal changes nothing. There is no operation that binds a thread to a process after it exists.

Reach reads this binding: a control capability over a thread reaches the thread's process, `kernel/verified/capability/reach.rs` `process_behind`, because whoever writes a thread's registers and starts it can make it invoke that process's capabilities. Made at retype, the binding grows nobody's reach: the new thread's capability has one holder, the caller, and the caller holds control over the process already, so the step discharges `Effect::Retype` as every retype does and the theorem keeps its wording, `design/theorem-over-reach/`: reach grows only by a grant from a holder or a retype from an untyped held.

Revoking the process capability's lineage unlinks the binding: the reference is dropped, the record's process cleared, and the thread is Inactive, unless it is Dead, or Running, in which case it gets a pending stop and its core's IPI and leaves the core at that core's next kernel entry, the mechanism a dead process's threads already use, `design/teardown-driven-by-retype/`. A thread without its process is inert, like a leaf or table page left behind by detach: it cannot start, it cannot be bound again, its registers stay readable and writable through its control capability while it is Inactive, it can be destroyed, and its block is retyped by the holder of its untyped once the capabilities naming it are gone. The kernel did not kill it; it withdrew the process, as revoke withdraws everything in a lineage, and the thread object survives for whoever holds it to read.

The rule this follows: a thread's process is the only binding reach reads. A binding reach reads is fixed when its object is made. A binding reach does not read, a thread's fault endpoint or its placement on a core, may be set, cleared and set again, since a fault message is a delivery and a placement is CPU time, `design/cpu-time-as-capability/`, and neither is an edge in reach. An object added later whose binding reach must read either takes that binding at retype, as a thread does, or, when the object exists before the authority it is bound to, as a boot-created device would with a device space, may be bound only by a holder who is its only holder, which a device has by construction since its capability does not derive, `design/sharing-by-derive/`. Both forms grow no reach; the first is used wherever it fits.

A destroyed thread whose binding still stands stays in reach's process map until its teardown unlinks the binding. That is harmless and deliberate: nothing can start a dead thread, so the model over-approximates what its controllers can do in the same way it counts a controlled dead process's holdings until they are claimed or revoked, and the teardown step only shrinks reach.

## Context

The thread and process objects landed with a separate bind operation: a holder of a thread's control capability and a process's control capability made the binding node under the process capability, and revoke's binding arm undid it. The reach view left the thread-to-process map empty, so the bind discharged a local effect while the model already said a thread's control reaches its process. Filling the map from the binding nodes, which the view must do before the thread leaves can be composed into the theorem, makes that bind grow the reach of every holder of a control capability naming the thread, and of everyone who controls such a holder, by what the process reaches. The binder cannot enumerate those holders. The plan was a third growth clause in the theorem, authorized by control over both objects. This record replaces that plan.

## Reasoning

What a separate bind does to the model is not unsound, but it is the one kind of growth the theorem would otherwise never admit. A retype grows the reach of whoever already reached the untyped. A delivery with a grant grows the reach of whoever controls the receiver's process, and the receiver chose to wait on that endpoint and named the slot. In both, authority moves upward from a subject's own act to those who control the subject. A bind grows the reach of every other holder of a capability to the same thread, by one holder's act, with no act and no consent by the others: it moves sideways between peers. A party that holds a thread capability and control over some process can bind the thread to it without knowing who else holds the thread, and has then granted them authority over that process unknowingly. Making the binding at retype removes the case: at that moment the caller is the only holder, and the meaning of a thread capability is fixed before anyone else can be given one. Derived holders never see their capability's reach change under them except to shrink, which revoke may always do.

Construction over a sole-holder bind. A bind refused unless the thread block has exactly one reference, which under `kernel/verified/capability/thread.rs` `threads_wf` is exactly one capability, gives the same property: the binder is the only holder. It keeps a second operation, puts a count-based refusal in the ABI, and keeps a blank thread as a normal state before its bind. Construction has one operation fewer, which is the smaller attack surface the project is for; it is how boot already makes the root thread, `design/root-process-at-boot/`; and it is the shape of the kernels that construct a thread in its task. What it gives up is moving a thread to another process after its binding was revoked. The design's recovery protocols already go through readable registers and new objects, `design/sharing-by-derive/`, pausing a process goes through its placements, not its process binding, and a detached leaf is not reattached either, so the loss is nothing the design relies on.

Why not kill. Marking the thread dead in the revoke arm would make revoke a kill, and the kernel never kills, `design/teardown-driven-by-retype/`. It would also break the invariant that a core's current thread is exactly the thread Running on it, `kernel/verified/capability/thread.rs` `current_wf`, since only that core changes a Running thread's state. The pending stop is the designed answer for both.

Why the binding is a node of its own, under the process capability. A thread has two lineages: its memory's, the untyped, which must reach it so that revoking the untyped reaches every node in blocks it covers, and its authority's, the process capability, which must reach it so that revoking that capability strips the process's threads. One node cannot have two parents, `design/revoke-by-depth-first-list/`, so the thread capability goes under the untyped and the binding under the process capability, and the retype makes both.

Proof shape. The landed `adds_object` predicate requires the new object to be no key of the process map, and `adds_node` requires that map unchanged. A retype into a process needs a widened predicate: the process map gains the new object mapped to the process, the caller holds a control capability naming that process, and no capability with the control right names the new block's base before the step. With it, every control chain in the post state is a chain in the pre state, since the one new link, from the caller through the new capability to the process, exists in the pre state through the process capability; and the new thread never becomes a control target of its own, so this lemma is simpler than the landed one for an unbound object. The last requirement is a fact the invariant does not yet state outright: a capability carrying the control right names a typed thread or process block. The retype itself is the landed thread retype followed by the landed bind's body in the same call, with its invariant lemma reused on the intermediate state.

Precedent, from published interfaces and not from code. Mach, NOVA and Zircon construct a thread in its task, protection domain or process. The L4 family and seL4 set a thread's address space and capability space after creation. Neither decides the question here: the reach theorem is stated per object over what each process holds now, so it is the theorem that makes the sideways growth visible, and this record that rules it out.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Admit the growth as a third clause.** Sound, but it is the only sideways growth in the theorem, it changes the theorem's one-line statement, and it lets a holder grant unknowingly; see above.
- **Kill on revoke.** The kernel never kills, and the current-thread invariant forbids another core writing a Running thread's state.
- **Bind only when the thread has one holder.** The same property by a dynamic check; one operation more and a count in the ABI. Kept as the shape for an object that exists before its binding.
- **Two keys to steer.** Requiring control over the process as well as the thread to write registers or start, so that a thread capability alone observes, stops and destroys, and binding is local. It coarsens the authority structure: resuming a thread would then need control over its process, which is the authority to claim everything the process holds, so a debugger that may resume is a debugger that may take. It also reopens the landed meaning of the control right, `design/process-holds-capabilities/`.
- **The binding under the thread's own capability, or under the untyped.** Revoking the process capability would then not strip the process's threads, so a holder whose process capability was withdrawn would keep running threads in it: revoke would not shrink reach.
- **A bind allowed once, on a never-bound thread.** Without the sole-holder check, holders of the blank thread's capability still see its reach grow at the bind; with it, it is the sole-holder bind.
- **Retype through the process capability, with the thread's capability placed in the process's table.** The creator must hold the capability to set registers and start the thread, so it lands in the caller's slot, and the operation is this one.
- **The process carried in the thread capability's payload.** A payload has one parent, so it cannot hold the process's reference or sit in the process capability's lineage.

## What this does not settle

- The pending stop and the IPI that moves a stopped thread off another core, `design/one-lock-bounded-steps/`.
- Whether reach will read a vCPU's nested table or a device's space, and which of the two forms above each then takes.
- Which argument word of the retype call carries the process slot.
- The distinction between a reference-holding node and a back-pointer cleared with it, `design/teardown-driven-by-retype/`.
