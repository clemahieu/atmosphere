# The theorem is per object over reach

## Decision

Direct reach of a process P: the objects its capabilities name, the capabilities descended from them, and the frames its address spaces map, a mapping being a hardware copy. A process sees a device space when it directly reaches the space or a device bound to it, and reaches through device spaces the space a device it directly reaches is bound to and each frame a space it sees maps: a device's accesses go through its space, so whoever reaches the device reaches what the space maps. `reach(P)` is the least set containing P and closed under direct reach and reach through device spaces. A process holding a control capability to a process, or to a thread of it, contributes what that process directly reaches, live or dead, until its slots are revoked: whoever writes a thread's registers and starts it can make it invoke its process's capabilities, so a thread's control capability reaches the thread's process, and the view reads which process that is from the thread's binding node. The closure makes control transitive: if S controls P and P controls Q, S reaches what Q directly reaches, since S can claim P's control over Q and repeat.

An operation changes objects only by a relation of its kind, not by general write. A frame in reach is read and written as its rights allow. An endpoint with send delivers at most five words and one derived capability to the matched receiver's frame and wakes it. A reply delivers once to its bound caller's frame. A notification sets bits and wakes a waiter. A delete or a revoke acts on a capability the caller commands: one it holds, or one held by a process it has a control capability to, the process's own and not one of its threads'. A delete removes that one capability. A revoke unlinks nodes only in the capability's lineage, which may lie in other processes' tables and spaces, and marks dead any object whose last reference it removed; its authority is the caller's when the step begins, since the lineage can include the control capability the caller named the slot through. Teardown of a dead object unlinks that object's own slots, mappings and bindings and their runs, which can be in other lineages, and wakes or stops what waited on it. Destroy, through a control capability, marks a thread or process dead. A move takes one node from one slot to another, leaving its place in its lineage unchanged. A claim is a move, through a control capability to a process, from that process's slot to the caller's table. A placement, through a control capability to a process, the process's own and not one of its threads', puts in a slot of that process's table a child of a capability the caller holds, or by a move a capability the caller commands: a grant through control, where an endpoint's is through a rendezvous. A map into a device space, under a capability to the space with the write right, of a frame capability the caller holds, is a grant to whoever sees the space, whom the mapper does not pick, `design/device-binding-under-the-space-capability/`; a map into a process's own space is local. Retype, on a block under a held untyped, types it if free, unlinks its hidden node, or does a slice of its teardown; a table leaf is typed into the caller's own table or into the table of a process the caller has a control capability to, the process's own and not one of its threads', and being no capability it grows nobody's reach; a thread is typed into a process the caller controls, so its control capability reaches that process from its first moment and no holder's reach grows, `design/thread-bound-at-retype/`. These change run queues and kernel lists, which no process can read directly. A step lists a thread as waiting on an object only when the thread is the stepping process's own and that process reaches the object, so a delivery or a signal wakes only a waiter whose process reached the object already; otherwise a process could list another tenant's thread on its private endpoint and grant it what it holds. A step leaves every thread it lists as waiting in the process it was in, for the same reason: rebinding a waiter to another process would leave that process's thread waiting on an object it never reached. A retype names a page its untyped covers, so the retyping process reaches the object before the step: a process whose reach is disjoint from it neither sees the retype change the object nor gains it, since it does not reach the untyped's block either.

The theorem: every step by a thread of P changes objects only by these relations over `reach(P)`; any user-mode access reads or writes only frames P's spaces map; and reach grows only by a grant from a holder, over an endpoint to the matched receiver, through control to the process controlled or through a device space to whoever sees it, or a retype from an untyped held. A retype creates something new rather than reaching something existing. Control over a process lets its controller take from the process and give to it. Taking moves what the process holds, which reach already counts, so a claim does not grow reach; deleting or revoking what the process holds only shrinks it. Giving grows the reach of that process and of every process that controls it, and by nothing the giver did not already reach, as an endpoint grant grows the receiver's. Two subsystems sharing an object still cannot reach each other's private objects unless one grants; isolation of fully disjoint subsystems follows. Its one-step form is `lemma_disjoint_step_stays_disjoint`: two processes whose reaches are disjoint, with no thread of what one reaches waiting on what the other reaches, keep both across any step by either that discharges its effect, given that every live capability covers the object it names (`covers_own_name`: a block of at least one page from an aligned base). Neither reach grows: the stepper regains only what it reached, and a grant to the other would need a shared waiter, a placement a shared control target, a share a device space both reach, a retype a shared untyped block. The waiting premise is needed: without it a delivery on the stepper's private endpoint may grant to the other's waiting thread.

Rights only narrow. Every right a capability has after a step, it had before, or the step made the capability and gave it a right its source had: a derive's parent, a move's node, a grant's capability, a placement's or a claim's node. A retype's new capability, naming the object typed, has the rights its kind allows. No other step gives a capability a right. Reach alone does not say this: a child of a send-only endpoint capability with the grant right covers only what its parent covers and grows nobody's reach, yet its holder could pass capabilities over an endpoint it was given only to send on, so a property of a state such as "no endpoint two subsystems share carries grant" would not survive a step. `keeps_rights` in the spec states the clause; a derive of grant from a send-only capability fails it.

What an object holds is in the view, so "changes objects only by a relation of its kind" constrains something. The view reads a frame's contents per page, from the page's permission in the kernel's frames: the bytes of the 4K page at a page-aligned address while the kernel holds the frame, and nothing otherwise. Two effects write pages, and each names how many: a retype zeroes the block it types, and a teardown that frees a frame returns its pages. `Effect::Retype` and `Effect::Teardown` carry the block's first address and its page count, `may_change` lets them change exactly those pages, and a retype is authorized only when its untyped covers the whole block, so the pages it zeroes are the untyped's. Every other effect changes no contents, and a step that writes a page it does not name fails to discharge.

Revocation only shrinks reach, so a parent's revocation needs no exemption. The old isolation proof's assumption, that memory is never revoked under verified user code, is gone with containers: verified user code must be specified knowing an ancestor can revoke mid-operation.

The spec is `kernel/verified/capability/reach.rs`; the view it is stated over is `kernel/verified/capability/view.rs`. Each capability operation's spec ensures `discharges` of its step, written to it rather than retrofitted.

## Context

`design/isolation-claim/` chose spatial isolation, first worded as two subsystems with disjoint capabilities cannot touch each other's memory or endpoints. Under `design/untyped-memory/` nearly every application shares frames with a pager or a driver, so two real subsystems are rarely disjoint and that wording says nothing about them. The commented-out proof in the old tree was that partition form, with a verified third party exempt.

## Reasoning

Reach constrains every syscall's spec from the start and covers the system the plan builds, where sharing is normal. Partition is closer to the old proof and smaller, but holds only for subsystems sharing nothing or with named exemptions.

A first wording of the theorem was found false for IPC, which writes the receiver's frame, and for bookkeeping a process can observe. The per-kind relations are what fixed it: the theorem is not "only reachable objects change" but "objects change only by the relation their kind allows, over reach".

## Alternatives rejected

- **Asking also that every capability covering a retype's object lie in the untyped's lineage, and that no space map it.** Derive narrows an untyped into siblings that each cover the same free page, so the lineage condition is false of the kernel; and a sibling's holder, or a process mapping the page, reaches the object before the step, so neither condition adds to isolation.
- **Partition between subsystems** with exemptions for a verified third party. Does not cover shared pagers, drivers or rings.
- **Contents keyed by object, the block a capability names.** The view would then read the block table, which nearly every operation changes, and a block's identity would move under retype and teardown; per-page contents read only the frames, which only retype and teardown write.
- **Counting a controlled process's reach only when dead.** Wider in wording than needed and no narrower in fact, since a controller can destroy and then claim at any time; and it would stop a controller that is not the pager from reading a live child's memory for a crash dump.

## What this does not settle

- The proof that composes the per-operation lemmas into the theorem, which lands with the last operation.
- A guest's reach is what its nested table maps, `design/vmm-over-svm/`.
